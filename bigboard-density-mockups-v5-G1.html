G1 — Spacing Iterations

Three takes on G1's spacing. The original used a fixed-column grid that left awkward gaps because content didn't fill its column. All three below switch to auto-sized layouts so content lives tight to itself. Each handles the leftover width differently: A distributes via flex-between, B packs it into a single "scoreboard" block on the right, C connects the two ends with a dotted leader line.

// G1.A Tight Two-Cluster

Each row is a flex container with space-between. The left cluster (chk · rank · pfp · name · pos) and the right cluster (team · ADP · verdict · ›) auto-size to their content. Inside each cluster, everything is tight (no gaps). The leftover space is one clean balanced gap in the middle — intentional negative space, not multiple awkward voids.

// G1.B Scoreboard Block

The right side becomes a visually distinct "scoreboard" block — a darker pill containing team · ADP · verdict, divided by thin lines. The left cluster (player info) and the right scoreboard now look like two design elements with intentional space between, not two halves of a stretched row. Eye knows: "stats live in the right block."

// G1.C Dotted Leader

Everything sits inline tight — chk · rank · pfp · name · then a dotted leader line bridging the gap to team · ADP · verdict · ›. Inspired by old yearbook/sports-almanac index layouts. The leader turns "void" into "visual connection," so the row feels resolved rather than gapped. Most compact, most editorial.
